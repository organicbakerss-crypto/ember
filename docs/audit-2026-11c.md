# Audit 3, November 2026: speed, gaps, clunky bits

I drove the built app with scripts and screenshots on the same big test library as audit 2:

- **The story:** 3 books, 90 chapters, about 196,000 words.
- **The world:** 300 entries.

AI was checked with a stand-in model. Nothing crashed and no screen logged an error.

Timings are from this test computer. A slower laptop or disk will be slower, but the proportions should hold.

**What's left to speed up:**

- **Find in the whole story (0.9 s).** It reads every chapter from disk on every search. Keeping the story text in memory (speed plan section 2) fixes this, and it also covers the inbox (0.26 s), the world (0.16 s) and summaries (0.18 s).
- **Opening a book freezes for a third of a second.** It's short, but you can feel it.
- **The very first start after a reboot took 18.7 s** because everything was read cold from disk. Every later start took 0.8 s. A packaged app would load fewer files, and this is worth timing on your own computer.

## 2. Broken or wrong (small)

1. **"World beside" / "Writing beside" uses bigger text** than every other button in the top bar.
2. **Settings → Import & export:** the "Chapter headings" and "Scene breaks" labels overlap.
3. **World mode sidebar** shows a horizontal scrollbar it doesn't need.
4. **The name catcher suggests direction words** ("New", "Old", "Lower", "South", "Upper") when they're part of place names. Real names like "North Harbour" or "Old Town" would trigger the same thing.

## 3. Hard to find

1. **The left icon rail has no labels.** Its eight icons (chapters, find, synopsis and notes, comments, history, diagnostics, world, AI) only say what they are on hover. Comments, Diagnostics and the AI pane are easy to miss.
2. **Opening the AI pane hides "World beside" and the Write/World switch** on the writing side. You have to close the AI to reach the world.
3. **The binder's right-click menu does a lot** (status, colour label, split, join, duplicate, test scenes, Copy for Royal Road, export), but nothing on screen hints that it's there.
4. **The series page has an unlabelled ⚙** next to "Import from Word…".
5. **The world's own menu (⋯)** sits far from everything else in World mode and is easy to overlook.

## 4. Clunky or hard to use

1. **Settings opens inside the current pane instead of on its own.** With the AI or the world beside, it's squeezed into half the window. In World mode it sits next to the world's sidebar, so there are two sidebars.
2. **The "Chapter ▾" and "POV" boxes above every chapter title** look like stray form fields on the page. They'd fit better as a quiet line under the title, or in the Synopsis & notes panel.
3. **The binder is a bare list of titles.** With 90 chapters there's no word count per chapter, no status colour you can see at a glance, and no way to filter it. Scrivener and Novelcrafter both show at least word counts.
4. **When → "Not on the timeline yet"** gives every event its own "Place it…" dropdown: 25 dropdowns in a column. It's hard to scan, and dragging is the only other way.
5. **How → Rulebook** shows a full-size card even for systems with nothing written ("No rules written yet"). 80 of them means a lot of scrolling.
6. **World search replaces the lens list** and pushes Where, Who, When and How down below the results.
7. **Settings → World is a long wall of checkboxes.** Twelve options in one run, with no grouping.
8. **The goals popup** opens over the middle of the page, not next to "Set goals".
9. **The bookshelf and series page** are mostly empty space at a normal window size. That's not wrong, but the series page could show more, such as the latest chapter written and progress toward goals.

## 5. What most writing apps have that this one doesn't

Compared with Scrivener, Novelcrafter, Atticus, Plottr, Campfire, World Anvil and Google Docs:

1. **Seeing what changed between two versions.** History can show and restore an old version, but not highlight the differences. Scrivener and Google Docs both do this. The side-by-side compare from Edit chapter already exists, so it could be reused.
2. **Pictures in a chapter.** The editor has no images. Royal Road chapters sometimes carry a picture, a map or a letter shown as an image. Only world entries have pictures.
3. **Word count per chapter in the binder** (see 4.3).
4. **Import from anything but Word.** Only .docx comes in. Markdown, plain text and Google Docs (via .docx, so it already works with an extra step) are the usual others.
5. **A thesaurus without AI.** "Better word" only exists with the AI on.
6. **Writing anywhere but this computer.** There's no phone or web version. Novelcrafter and Google Docs win here. This is a big job and outside the plan ("Arch and Mac first"), but it's the most noticeable gap next to web apps.
7. **Sharing a chapter for feedback.** Beta readers can't comment the way they can in Google Docs. Export works, but their comments don't come back in.
8. **Choosing your own keyboard shortcuts.** Scrivener and Word let you; here the list is fixed.
9. **Updates by themselves.** You `git pull` and rebuild. A packaged app with updates is standard elsewhere.

Things you already turned down aren't listed again: the outline/corkboard, writing stats and deadlines, and AI-written scenes.

## 6. Suggested order

1. **Quick fixes (one session):** all of section 2, plus the series-page ⚙ label and the goals popup position.
2. **Hard to find:** labels on the icon rail (shown when the window is wide enough, or a setting); keep World beside reachable while the AI is open.
3. **Clunky:** Settings as its own window or full screen; a tidier chapter header; word counts and status colours in the binder, with a filter box.
4. **Speed:** the story in memory (section 2 of the speed plan), which makes story-wide Find instant.
5. **Missing features, pick from:** compare versions, pictures in chapters, Markdown/text import, a thesaurus without AI.
6. **Bigger and later:** packaging and updates, phone or web access, sharing for feedback.
